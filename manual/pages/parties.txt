Parties: a controller, its runners and its clients trust each other by exchanging cards.

A controller, a runner and a client each have two private keys, in a file of
the config that only its owner can read, and a card that holds the two public
keys. Exchanging cards is the only way two of them trust each other. A runner
can trust several controllers, and a client one.

How it works
------------

1. `corbie runner card` prints the runner's card.
2. `corbie controller enrol <card> --tags gpu` saves the card in the store,
   and prints the controller's card and the store's address.
3. `corbie runner trust <card> <address> --ceiling exec` saves both in the
   runner's config.

A client does the same with `corbie client card`, `enrol --ceiling` and
`corbie client trust`. With `-` in place of the card, each command reads
standard input, so the three join in a pipe (`corbie manual served-directory`).
On one computer `corbie init` does all of it.

A card
------

  corbie-card:runner:gpu-runner:-PQKFRmtpohO…:xvHYPlHfQUK…:Jtv0CPiSeXG…

A card is one line: the kind, the name, the public key for signatures, the
public key for encryption, and the owner's signature of these. `card`, `enrol`
and `trust` print each card's fingerprint on standard error, such as
`3716-bae3-71ba-ff4d`, for two people to compare.

A name is at most 64 letters, digits, `.`, `-` and `_`, and cannot be changed
after its keys are created. It stays bound to the keys it was first trusted
with, also after `remove`, and a card with other keys under it is refused.
With `--replace` the new card takes the name, with none of the old card's tags
or ceiling.

Ceilings and tags
-----------------

  a controller gives a client a ceiling   the most any run that client starts may be allowed
  a runner gives a controller a ceiling   the most that controller's jobs may be allowed on the runner
  a controller gives a runner tags        a job with `tags:` goes to a runner that has every tag it names

A ceiling is permission entries with commas between them, such as
`network,disk`. With an empty ceiling, jobs are allowed no permission.

`enrol <name>` and `trust <name>` set the tags or the ceiling of one already
trusted or removed. `--ceiling` and `--tags` replace what was set, and
`--allow` and `--deny` add one entry. A change needs no restart.

Tags and a ceiling stay as they are unless a flag changes them. `--tags none`
and `--ceiling none` remove them.

    corbie init --store ~/corbie --ceiling all --name laptop
    corbie controller enrol laptop-client --allow net:api.github.com
    corbie runner trust laptop-controller --allow exec:git
    corbie runner trust laptop-controller --deny 'fs.read:/home/**'
    corbie controller parties
    corbie controller remove laptop-runner

Other commands
--------------

  corbie controller card --store <address>   creates the controller's keys if it has none; prints its card
  corbie controller parties                  lists the runners and clients the controller trusts
  corbie controller remove <name>            the controller stops trusting a runner or a client
  corbie runner remove <controller>          the runner stops taking that controller's jobs
  corbie client remove <controller>          the client stops using that controller

A viewer
--------

A viewer has no keys. Its `corbie ls` and `corbie show <id>` list every
client's runs with their status, and cannot read an input, an output or a
record.

  corbie client trust <card> <address>   on a config with no client keys, sets up a viewer
  corbie controller policy --viewer      on a bucket, prints the policy for the viewer's access key

A served directory has no viewers, because every connection to it proves a
private key.
