Schedules: a statechart waits for a time with `after:` and then starts the work as a job.

Corbie has no scheduler: recurring work is a statechart with two states.

    # nightly.yaml
    statechart: nightly
    initial: idle

    states:
      idle:
        after: { "18:00": working }
      working:
        job: { script: ./work.js }
        done: idle
        failed: idle

    // work.js
    log("the nightly work")

    corbie init --store ~/corbie --ceiling all
    corbie run controller runner &
    corbie submit nightly.yaml
    corbie show <id>

At 18:00 UTC the statechart moves to `working`, and back to `idle` when the
job ends. Without `failed: idle`, a job that fails ends the run as failed and
the schedule stops. `corbie show <id>` prints the run as `running`, with
`waiting on 1 timer`, until `corbie cancel <id>` ends it.

`after:` takes a duration or a time of day.

  90s, 48h, 7d             a duration, from when the statechart enters the state
  "18:00"                  a time of day in UTC, due at its next occurrence
  "mon 09:00"              a time on one day of the week
  "09:00 Europe/London"    a time of day in a named time zone

No job runs while the statechart waits. A timer that came due while no
controller was running is delivered when one starts. A running controller
delivers a timer at most this late.

  started with corbie run     its --interval (default 2s)
  as a serverless function    one minute
